# Positioning Against Hint (Martha Stewart's Home Intelligence)

Founder-ready notes for the deck, the demo, and investor conversations. Derived from a teardown of the Chez deck plus 11 Hint product screens (May 2026). Companion to the in-app product work in the same phase.

## The one-line frame

**Hint tells you. Chez does it.** Everything else is detail.

## Who Hint is (so the room knows you did the work)

Martha Stewart's home app. The edge is brand, distribution, and editorial trust: Martha owns "the home" in the mind of exactly your buyer, which is a CAC advantage you cannot copy. Underneath the brand the software is thin and mostly white-labeled: the chat is a wrapped LLM, the Shop is white-labeled Updater (every step carries "you agree to the Updater terms"), and the Library is a file vault auto-seeded with MLS photos and zero real documents. At every layer it stops at the list. Every task row ends in a "CHAT" button. Even the Shop ends with you clicking Select and signing up with the provider yourself.

The honest threat is not their features. It is the day Martha's team white-labels a human concierge the way they white-labeled Updater.

---

## A1. The CHAT-button contrast (your sharpest slide)

The single most damaging fact about Hint is visible on every screen: the only action on a task is "CHAT." They built a beautiful, brand-rich way to hand you back your own to-do list.

**Suggested treatment for the competitive slide (slide 7):** a literal side-by-side.
- Left: a real Hint screenshot, the wall of 50 tasks, every row ending in CHAT.
- Right: a single Chez proposal card (vendor, quote, fair-market band, Approve / Counter / Decline).
- Caption: **"Theirs ends in a chat. Ours ends in done."**

Let the screenshot make the argument. You do not need to say Hint is bad. You need the room to see that their finish line is a conversation and yours is a completed job.

---

## A2. Defend the affiliate fork on purpose (slide 5)

Hint proves affiliate commerce is real revenue for this exact audience: Updater-powered internet and electricity switching. An investor who has seen Hint will ask the obvious question, so answer it before they ask.

**The question:** "Hint monetizes internet and electricity switching. Why are you leaving that on the table?"

**The answer:** Affiliate revenue trains the customer to see you as a middleman taking a cut, not an advocate spending their money well. For a household that hands you the keys, the vendor, and the bill, the instant they smell a kickback you lose the relationship that is the entire company. You make more per home on the coordination take-rate, and you keep the trust that compounds into the next job and the neighbor's referral.

**Deck:** keep the existing line "We deliberately avoid affiliate kickbacks and insurance. Trust is the product." Consider adding one sub-line beneath it: **"Hint takes the kickback. Chez takes the relationship."**

---

## A3. Reframe the moat as depth plus density, not features

A feature checklist invites the response "Martha could build that." The honest and stronger frame is that the moat is not on the feature list.

**Suggested language:** "The threat is not Hint's product, which is thinner than ours. It is Martha's brand plus the day they white-label a concierge. Our defense is not a feature they can copy in a sprint. It is the operating depth of actually running homes (the desk, the vetted vendors, the home data we already hold) and the density flywheel in a corridor where every job recruits a vendor and every neighbor refers. Brand wins the first install. Depth and density keep the home."

This is more credible than claiming a longer feature list, and it is the true read.

---

## A4. Open the demo on the proposal card

The slide-3 "your entire job, in one card" is your most differentiated artifact, and in the live product it is currently buried until a real request resolves (the in-app work in this phase surfaces it earlier).

**In the room:** open every demo on the proposal card, not the dashboard. One card: vendor, quote, fair-market band, Approve / Counter / Decline. It is the fastest way to make someone feel the 95%. The dashboard shows what you track. The card shows what you do. Lead with what you do.

---

## Traps to avoid

- **Do not try to out-brand Martha.** Unwinnable head-on. Concede the brand point and pivot to depth and density.
- **Do not disparage Hint.** The CHAT-button contrast is devastating on its own. Stay gracious; let the screenshot work.
- **Do not overclaim AI.** Hint is the AI-chat home app. Your wedge is the opposite: a real person does the work. Leaning on "our AI" blurs the one line that separates you. The AI is your leverage behind the desk, not the pitch.
