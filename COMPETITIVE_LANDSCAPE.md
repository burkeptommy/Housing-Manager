# Competitive Landscape: The Slide-7 Set, Read One by One

Companion to HINT_COMPETITIVE_RESPONSE.md. One tight read on each competitor named on the deck's competitive slide: their real edge, and exactly where they stop. The throughline holds across all twenty. They organize, remind, or list. Chez does the work.

## What changed since you built the deck (worth knowing before the next investor call)

- **Hint is now real and well funded.** Martha Stewart's Hint launched in May 2026 with a $10M seed led by Slow Ventures (with Tusk Venture Partners, Amplo, Energy Impact Partners, Hannah Grey, Brian Kelly of The Points Guy, and Montauk Capital's venture studio, which incubated it). iOS ships summer 2026; today it is a desktop and web beta. That is double your raise behind a brand you cannot out-spend on awareness. Treat Hint as the headline competitor, not a footnote in a list of five.
- **Hint is going into home insurance.** Reported alongside the launch. This sharpens your slide 5 from a hypothetical into a provable contrast: Hint monetizes the home through affiliate switching and insurance, the exact levers you deliberately decline. "Trust is the product, no kickbacks, no insurance" now has a named example sitting across the table.
- **Centriq shut down (January 2025).** The category's purest "scan your appliance, store the manual" app retired, was sold, and deleted user data; Homer absorbed the refugees. It is a live logo on your slide and a dead company in reality. Use it as evidence that organize-only models do not survive.

## Task Managers (you build the list, you do every task)

- **Cozi.** Edge: the default shared family calendar, tens of millions of users, free, color-coded per person. Where it stops: it is a calendar and a set of lists. No home, no systems, no vendors, nothing done. Ad-supported.
- **Maple.** Edge: a family mental-load organizer (shared calendar, family inbox, chores, meals, grocery) with an AI "Maple Assistant" that turns school emails into tasks. Where it stops: the assistant organizes, it does not act, and the domain is kids and logistics, not home systems, vendors, or maintenance.
- **Skylight.** Edge: a hardware beachhead, the Skylight Calendar screen on the kitchen wall, now with an AI sidekick. Where it stops: family scheduling and chores. A device, not a home operator. Nothing gets coordinated or completed.
- **OurHome.** Edge: free, gamified chores and rewards for kids. Where it stops: a chore chart. No home intelligence of any kind.
- **Todoist.** Edge: the best general-purpose task manager, cross-platform, powerful, loved by power users. Where it stops: a blank to-do app with zero home domain. Every task is yours to define and yours to do.

## Proactive Maintenance (tells you what is due, doing it is still on you)

- **Hint.** Edge: Martha Stewart's brand, editorial trust, web reach, an affiliate Shop, and insurance on the way. Where it stops: every task ends in a CHAT button and even the Shop ends with you signing up yourself; the software underneath is thin and largely white-labeled. Full teardown in HINT_COMPETITIVE_RESPONSE.md.
- **Oply.** Edge: the closest on paper to your loop, with predictive AI, a home health score, and one-click pro booking and payments. Where it stops: one-click booking is still you booking, not a person handling it, and the real vetted-pro network is essentially one state deep (Tennessee) behind a "nationwide" app. The density problem you name on slide 6, playing out live.
- **Dwellin.** Edge: a tidy home binder, a maintenance calendar, an asset tracker, a coverage plan, and a sustainability and carbon angle that is genuinely differentiated copy. Where it stops: it tracks, reminds, and points you at a pro directory. You still coordinate and do.
- **HomeKeep.** Edge: the closest competitor on the one axis that matters, because it actually does the work. A dedicated concierge catalogs and registers your appliances, and on higher plans a specialist performs twice-yearly seasonal maintenance, with a strong homebuilder channel. Where it stops: the scope is bounded to scheduled preventative maintenance and appliances, sold largely through builders. It is not "hand off anything in the home," and it is not built for the HNW estate. Your breadth (any vendor, quote, project, or errand) and your audience are the wedge against the one rival that also picks up a wrench.
- **BrightNest.** Edge: friendly maintenance tips and reminders, once Angi-affiliated. Where it stops: tips and reminders, and it appears long dormant. A reminder engine, never an operator.

## Vendor Marketplaces (a directory of pros you vet, hire, and manage)

- **Angi (Angie's List plus HomeAdvisor).** Edge: the largest pro network and review corpus, with enormous lead-generation scale. Where it stops: it sells your request to several pros at once and steps away. You field the calls, vet, hire, and manage. The lead-broker model is structurally the opposite of a trusted advocate, which is your entire position.
- **Thumbtack.** Edge: a broad request-to-quote marketplace, fast to surface options. Where it stops: you sift the quotes, vet, and manage every job. Transactional, with no ongoing ownership of your home.
- **Houzz.** Edge: design inspiration plus a pro directory plus renovation commerce, strong for big remodels. Where it stops: project and design centric, not ongoing upkeep. You still hire and manage the pro.
- **Porch.** Edge: home-services lead generation bundled with mover services, now largely an insurance and data company. Where it stops: lead gen and cross-sell, not coordination. The consumer product is secondary to the B2B and insurance pivot.
- **TaskRabbit.** Edge: on-demand Taskers for small jobs, same-day, IKEA-grade convenience. Where it stops: gig labor you book and supervise task by task. No home intelligence, no continuity, no ownership.

## Home Management (scan, track, store)

- **Centriq.** Edge: snap an appliance label and get the manual, warranty, and recalls, with the best equipment-intelligence UX in the category. Where it stops: it shut down in January 2025, sold and data-deleted, users pushed to Homer. The purest organize-only product, and it could not survive on organizing alone.
- **Homer.** Edge: a beautiful, Apple-featured home inventory and maintenance app out of Sweden, which absorbed Centriq's users and now has scale. Where it stops: it is a gorgeous binder. It remembers and reminds, it does not act.
- **HomeZada.** Edge: the most comprehensive home-management suite, covering inventory, finances, maintenance, and projects, for the spreadsheet-loving power user. Where it stops: data-entry heavy and prosumer. Depth of tracking, not an ounce of doing.
- **Encircle.** Edge: fast property and contents documentation, but built for insurance claims and restoration pros. Where it stops: it is a B2B field tool for adjusters and restorers. A different buyer and a different job than a homeowner concierge.
- **Nines.** Edge: the premium option, household and estate management software for the ultra-wealthy and the staff who run their homes. Where it stops: as your deck already says, it is software for a six-figure estate manager you hire first. The work still scatters across a dozen vendors, with no one inside the tool to do it. Chez is the work, with no one to hire.

## The white space

Across all twenty, exactly two even attempt to do the work: HomeKeep (narrowly, scheduled preventative maintenance sold through builders) and Oply (self-serve booking, one state deep). None combines the three things at once that define Chez: done-for-you breadth across any vendor, task, routine, system, and project; a real person on the desk rather than a chatbot; and a premium, density-first wedge into the HNW home. That intersection is empty, and it is exactly where you sit. Everyone else hands you a better list. You hand them back their Saturday.
